What the numbers say

How A* and AO* work, and how AO* is modelled

A* expands the node with the lowest f(n) = g(n) + h(n), where g is the distance driven so far. With an admissible h (never over-estimates) the route it returns is the shortest one.

AO* works on an AND-OR graph. P(u→v) means “get the package from u to v”. It is solved either by driving the road u–v (an OR choice) or by picking a waypoint w and solving both P(u→w) and P(w→v) (an AND arc). Splitting repeats until every piece is a single road. Costs are revised bottom-up: f(n) = min over arcs [ arc cost + Σ f(children) ].

Heuristics. H1 is the straight-line distance. H2 takes the larger of H1 and a landmark bound (three landmark junctions with exact road distances, shown as purple diamonds), so it is tighter but still never over-estimates. H3 inflates H2 by 1.6 and may return longer routes. H0 gives no guidance.

AO* settings. Only the K most promising waypoints are generated per step, and an arc is pruned when even its optimistic estimate cannot beat the direct road. A waypoint is rejected if it would close a cycle, because AO* needs an acyclic graph.
